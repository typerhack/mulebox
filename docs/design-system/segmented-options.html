The segmented choice: three ways to show the chosen part

Of the dashboard on 2026-11-13: the pattern [of the choice] in the machine settings is looking pretty bad. I don't like it at all. Especially the underline border, it's pretty bad. The window around it stays as it is.

Each option is drawn where the control is used today: the Priority panel of a node's settings window, the project levels of the Order panel, and the period switch of the Statistics page, at desktop size and in a 390 px frame with 44 px targets. None has an underline. Every control here works: click a part, or focus one and use the arrow keys.

A

A tile in a track

The group is one grey track, and the chosen part is a tile outlined in ink that sits in it, like the knob of a slide switch.

Chosen: tile edge in --text-1 on a --surface-3 track, 13.9:1 light and 10.9:1 dark, plus a heavier word. No divider lines. No new token; in the dark theme the tile is darker than its track, and a lighter tile would need one new token for it.

B

Ink fill

The outlined group stays, and the chosen part is filled with the text colour, its word reversed: the darkest thing in the row, so it is read first.

Chosen: --text-1 fill beside --surface-1 parts, 17.4:1 light and 14.6:1 dark, the word in --surface-1 at the same ratio. No teal, so it never competes with Save changes. No new token.

C

Radio marks

No box at all: every part has a ring before its word, and the chosen ring is filled in teal, as on a paper form. Stacked rows line up as columns of rings.

Chosen: a filled --accent ring against empty --border-strong rings, 5.8:1 light and 8.6:1 dark on a panel, plus a heavier word. The quietest of the three, and the widest: about 30 px more per row. No new token.

+

For all three

Keyboard focus is the page's own 2 px teal ring, 2 px off the part, so it is never the same mark as the chosen state. Hover darkens the word and shows where a press would land.

Any of the three retires --chosen-line from the segmented choice; tabs keep their own line. A and B show the chosen part in ink, so the design system's sentence "the chosen part in --accent" would change with them; C keeps it. A part keeps its width when chosen, which it does not today.
